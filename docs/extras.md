# Ventilation, fan, wall mounting, labels

## Ventilation

Open **Ventilation** in the Openings card and press the faces to vent in
the *Faces* row (top, bottom, front, back, left, right — any number; press
again to take one off). The vents are on while a face is pressed; the rest
of the settings show then.

| Control | Range | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pattern | slots / hexagons / holes | slots | slots are rounded, 4 × their width long; hexagons and holes sit on a staggered grid |
| Slots | horizontal / vertical | vertical | |
| Size | 1–10 mm | 2 | slot width, hole diameter, hexagon across its flats |
| Pitch | size + 0.8 … 30 mm | 4 | centre to centre |
| Margin | 1–40 mm | 4 | from the face's rounded edges |

The pattern fills the flat part of each face and is centred on it. Vents
are left out where they would do harm: across the band where the lid skirt
sits, under a standoff or a rail (a vent there would open it), inside a port
opening. On a cylinder the four sides are the round wall seen from four
directions.

## Fan seat

**Fan seat**: pick its face in the *Face* row (*none* — no fan). A round
opening of the fan's size − 2 mm and four screw holes on the standard
square (25 → 20, 30 → 24, 40 → 32, 50 → 40, 60 → 50 mm), shifted from the face's centre by X / Y. Screw holes default to
3.2 mm. The panel warns when the fan does not fit the flat part of the face.

## Wall mount

*Against the wall* picks the face the case rests on: bottom (the default),
back, front, left, right or top — the console's slope is not offered. The
lid's face may be chosen too: then whatever holds the lid holds the case,
and the panel warns (more sharply on snaps, which let go). A file saved
before this choice hung by its floor, or by its back wall when the floor was
the lid, and opens so.

- **keyholes** — two keyholes, *Spacing* apart, through the face against the
  wall: a hole for the screw head (2 × screw + 1 mm) and a 6 mm slot for the
  shank (screw + 0.6 mm), running up from the head hole on a wall. Hang the
  case on two screws and slide it down. Keep them away from standoffs (the
  panel warns).

  *Keyholes: through / closed.* A through keyhole opens into the case. A
  closed one is a blind pocket the screw head slides in, in a pad standing
  out of the face (the plate alone is too thin for it), so the case stays
  closed: the head hole and the slot from outside, the head's channel
  behind the slot, 1 mm of the plate left towards the cavity. On the bottom
  the pads go to its far end and two feet of the same height to the near
  end, so the case stands level on four; on a short case each pad and its
  foot merge into one strip. On any other face there are no feet. Pads on
  the face the body prints on (the one opposite the lid) stand on the bed,
  and the plate between them needs supports (the panel says so). A
  cylinder's round wall has no flat place for a pad: there the keyholes go
  through. Vents are left out under pads and feet. A standoff right above a
  pocket would open it (its hole goes into the plate) — the panel warns.
- **ears** — tabs flush with the face against the wall (at floor level when
  the bottom is), 10 × 8 mm, as thick as that face's plate, with a hole for
  the screw (+ 0.4 mm), standing out of the walls next to it. *Ears*: 1–4.
  *On the walls*: a pair of opposite walls next to the face — left and
  right, front and back, top and bottom, whichever the face has (an odd ear
  goes to the first wall: 3 = 2 + 1) — or one wall of your choice. *Along
  the wall* (when a wall gets two or more): centred, *Spacing* apart, or at
  the ends — spread out to the corners. Ears stay on the flat part of a
  wall, clear of the rounded corners and edges.

  An ear that would hang in its part's print pose — on a side wall of a
  body printed opening up, say — gets a 45° gusset back to its wall on its
  lower side, so it prints without supports. An ear on a wall the lid's part
  crosses stays on the body's part, 1 mm off the parting; when the body's
  part is too short for it, the panel says so. An ear whose root is in the
  lid's wall goes with the lid (the lid then holds the case — the panel
  warns), and an ear standing out of the face its part prints on would be
  under the bed (the panel warns: choose other walls or another lid face).
  A cylinder hangs by its top or bottom only: its ears go evenly around,
  the first towards the first wall named.

## Labels

**+ Add label** puts a line of text on a face — up to 8 labels, each with
its own face, text and place, and a ✕ to remove it. A new label goes on the
lid's face and says the name of the first board no label names yet (edit
or clear it). A file saved with the one label of before opens as a list of
one (none, if that label was off).

| Control | Range | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Face | top, bottom, front, back, left, right | the lid's | any flat face of the body or the lid; a cylinder's top and bottom only (a flat relief does not lie on a round wall); a console's top is its slope |
| Text | up to 40 characters | — | Latin, Cyrillic and Hebrew |
| Font | Rubik / Oswald | Rubik | Oswald has no Hebrew letters; they come out in Rubik |
| Size | 3–30 mm | 6 | the height of a capital letter |
| Raised | on / off | off | off — engraved |
| Depth | 0.4–1.5 mm | 0.6 | an engraving never goes deeper than the face's plate − 0.6 mm |
| Place | centre / an edge / a corner | centre | a 3 × 3 grid, as seen from outside the face |
| From the edge | 0–30 mm | 3 | an edge or a corner only: how far the label's box keeps from the face's rounded edges and corners |
| Shift X, Y | −100 … 100 mm | 0 | a fine shift from the chosen place; a new place resets it |
| Turn | 0 / 90 / 180 / 270° | 0 | quarter turns; *Angle* takes any other |
| Angle | −180 … 180° | 0 | |

The text reads from outside the face. Against an edge or in a corner the
label's box — turned, if it is — stays *From the edge* inside the face's
flat part. A label on a side wall that the lid's part crosses is cut with
the parts: an engraving in both, a raised label split between them, each
piece stopping 0.2 mm short of the parting. The panel warns, naming the
label, when it runs off the flat part of its face, over a screw head on the
lid, across the line between the body and the lid, over an opening in its
face, raised on the face its part prints on (the letters would be the first
layer — engrave it or move it), or over another label on the same face.
Fonts load only when a label has text.
