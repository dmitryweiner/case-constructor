# Keyboards

A keyboard is the board the case is built around: its keys come through the
lid. The catalog has keyboards sold as boards in a group of their own
(**Keyboards** in the picker; search finds them by "keyboard", "keypad" or
"macropad").

## Hung from the lid, or on the floor

A keyboard hangs from the top when added, like a display (see
[Displays](displays.md)): the standoffs or rails come down from the top
plate, the plate lies over it, the keys come up through it. On a console it
hangs from the slope.

A keyboard whose mounting holes are among its keys stands on the floor
instead: a post from the lid would run into the keys (the MacroPad's holes
are at its switches' edges; one of the BBQ20KBD's holes is under its
keyboard). On the floor the case's ceiling comes down to it — the gap
(0.3 mm) above what the plate lies on — rather than the top margin, so the
keys reach through the lid. Added, it stands just high enough for the least
case (20 mm); lower, the panel says how far the lid lies above its switches.

## Over the keys

**Over the keys** in the board's box:

- **A window** — one opening over the whole key field, chamfered 45°
  outwards like a screen's (the same **Chamfered window** toggle). The
  field rises into it: the plate lies on what is outside the field. The
  CardKB's window covers its whole face, so its key legends show.
- **A hole per key** — the lid is the switch plate (14 mm holes for MX
  switches, the KeebDeck's faceplate). The plate lies on the keys' base,
  between them. Needs the entry's key rows.
- **Printed caps** — a hole per key and a cap in each: a stem over the
  button, standing 1 mm out of the lid, and a flange under the lid wider than
  the hole, so it cannot fall out. For keys too low to reach with a finger
  (the CardKB's tact switches stand 1.75 mm). The flange lies on the key, the
  plate the gap above it. The caps print with the lid: in the Lid STL and in
  Both they lie flange down in rows next to the lid; in the view they stand
  in their holes (exploded, over the lid).
  **Caps** picks their shape: round, or square with rounded corners
  (**Corner radius**, 0.3 mm at least — never sharp); the holes follow the
  stems' outline, grown by the fit.

**Key fit** is the room around the window, or around each key (or cap stem)
in its hole: the field's own clearance by default, 0.25 mm for caps.

The panel says how far the key tops stand out of the lid, or how deep they
sit in a window. It warns when:

- the key tops are under the face with a hole per key (a finger cannot
  press a key at the bottom of a hole): open a window, use caps, make the
  top thinner (**Floor and ceiling**) or the gap 0;
- the web between two holes is thinner than 0.8 mm: lower the fit;
- a cap's flange reaches less than 0.4 mm past its hole: lower the fit;
- a floor keyboard's lid lies above its switches' plate line: raise the
  board by the amount it names.

## A keypad's flange

A silicone keypad (the KeebDeck's) is wider than its keys: its flange lies
under the plate, which holds it down, and a rim around it locates it — the
display's glass seat. Such a keyboard hangs pressed against the plate (gap
0, no warning).

## Connectors

A connector that stands taller than what the plate lies on opens from behind
its body, through the plate (the KeebDeck's USB-C beside its keypad). The
CardKB's Grove socket stands up into its window, and its plug gets a slot in
the lid 12 mm long beside the board, through which the cable goes into the
case: the wall stays whole, and the case keeps room for the slot.

## Rests

A keyboard held at only one or two holes (the BBQ20KBD's are on a diagonal)
rocks under a key press. A rest stands under each corner with no hole within
20 mm: a column from the floor up to the PCB, clear of the parts underneath —
touching a board on the floor, 0.2 mm short of a hung one. A rest longer
than 40 mm, or with another board in its way, is not built, and the panel
says so.
